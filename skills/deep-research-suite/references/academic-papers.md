# Academic / literature research

## When to load

Use for papers, reviews, experiments, algorithms and scientific claims. For a **systematic review**, capture an explicit protocol, databases, dates and reproducible search strings; do not call an informal narrative review systematic.

## Procedure

1. Define population/question, inclusion/exclusion criteria, venues and time range before screening; capture exact search dates and language coverage.
2. Discover records using publisher databases (ACM, IEEE, journals), DOI registries (Crossref), scholarly indexes (OpenAlex/Semantic Scholar), Google Scholar and field-specific sources. For preprints (arXiv, bioRxiv), flag non-peer-reviewed status when applicable.
3. Deduplicate DOI/title/author/date identities carefully; do not collapse peer-reviewed and preprint versions without documenting the relation.
4. Read relevant original methods, datasets, assumptions, baseline, sample size, reported uncertainty, limitations and replication materials. Avoid using abstracts or citations alone as evidence for a nuanced conclusion.
5. Check corrections, retractions and version history where credible findings depend on the paper. Do not treat citation count, impact factor or h-index as a substitute for study validity.
6. Preserve contradictory findings and differences in protocol; when combining quantitative results, verify comparability rather than averaging incompatible measurements.
7. Cite the particular paper version/DOI and passage, not only a search index record. Record inaccessible full texts as limitations rather than infer their results.
